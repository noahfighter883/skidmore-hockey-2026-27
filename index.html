Data notes

Where the numbers come from

Each export is a spreadsheet of skater statistics for the season so far, with per-game averages for each player. Season totals here are the per-game average multiplied by games played and rounded, so a total can be off by one. A single game is the difference between two exports. Points are goals plus assists. This page is through game , from the export of .

The file has skaters only. It has no goalie statistics, opponent, final score, injuries, line combinations or zone starts. Team goals, shots and expected goals are the sum of the skaters. Whether a goal went against the team is only visible as goals against while a skater was on the ice.

Ice time looks under-logged

Last season's exports added up to about 59 minutes of ice time per 60-minute game (the sum of every skater's minutes divided by five). Game 1 adds up to about 41 minutes, while shots and goals look like a normal game. If part of the game was not logged, then ice time, points per 60 and per-game counts like entries and breakouts will read low or high. Shot share, pass accuracy and faceoff percentages are ratios and hold up better. The page checks this on every update and shows a warning on the Overview tab when the total is under 55 minutes.

Choices made

Shot share is the team's shot attempts divided by all shot attempts while the player was on the ice, calculated from the attempt counts. Last season's figures in the comparison come from the same spreadsheet column that last year's dashboard used, so a small difference in method is possible. Rate charts leave out players with fewer than half the team's games.

Green and orange compare against an even line: zero for margins, 50% for shot share and faceoffs. Gaps smaller than a noise threshold are drawn grey. The threshold shrinks as games are added, so early-season charts are mostly grey on purpose. Pass accuracy is compared with the team median and is not colored, because being below the median is not the same as being bad.

Expected goals (xG) and the on-ice margin come from the data provider's model, not from this analysis. Power-play and penalty-kill time are empty in the game 1 export even though penalties were drawn, so those two charts stay hidden until the columns fill in.

Two sets of faceoff numbers

Faceoffs can come from two places. The data export is the same spreadsheet as everything else. The hand count is tallied at the rink and typed into data/faceoffs-hand.json. Wherever the page shows a faceoff number, it says which one it is.

How to read the luck language

When a player scores fewer goals than expected, the usual explanation is variance and the expected-goals value tends to be a better guide to the next game than the actual total. That is a tendency, not a rule, and the first few games are a very small sample. The same goes for plus-minus compared with the on-ice chance margin.
